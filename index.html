Guidance

What hospital will actually do

The "at hospital / convey / alternative" lines are orientation, not a discharge pathway — non-conveyance sits with JRCALC, your local pathways and your scope of practice. Each card opens the live source page so you always get the current version; nothing is copied into this app. Two cards are marked "not NICE" — NICE publishes no DKA algorithm, so those point at the JBDS and BSPED guidelines that UK hospitals actually work to.

Head injury imaging

NICE NG232 — CT head and cervical spine

Criteria from the NICE NG232 imaging algorithm (2023), reproduced for clinical use with attribution — source PDF. These are the criteria the emergency department applies, so use them to anticipate what hospital will do, not as a discharge pathway. Two items need a hospital clock — GCS at 2 hours, and 4 hours of observation in children — so if you cannot exclude them on scene, that is a convey.

Blood results

Typical adult reference ranges

Typical UK adult ranges in SI units. Reference ranges are set by the reporting laboratory and vary between them — the range printed on the report always wins over this list. Children, pregnancy and some chronic conditions have different ranges entirely, and a result inside the range never rules out illness.

Milestones are a normal range, not a deadline — most have months of spread either side and premature babies are assessed on corrected age.     STEMI thresholds

    • New ST elevation at the J point in 2 contiguous leads.
    • V2–V3: 2 mm or more in men aged 40+, 2.5 mm in men under 40, 1.5 mm in women.
    • All other leads: 1 mm or more.
    • Posterior: ST depression in V1–V3 — confirm with 0.5 mm elevation in V7–V9 (1 mm in men under 40).
    • Right ventricle: 0.5 mm elevation in V3R–V4R (1 mm in men under 30).
    • LBBB or paced: ST changes are unreliable — use Sgarbossa criteria.

    ECG paper

    1 small square = 40 ms and 0.1 mV. 1 large square = 200 ms and 0.5 mV. Standard speed 25 mm/s, gain 10 mm/mV.
